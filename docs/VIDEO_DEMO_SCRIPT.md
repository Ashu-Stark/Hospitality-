## Voice-over only (read straight through, about 270 words)

> Six a.m. in Mussoorie. The season has started.
> Every room is booked. Two housekeepers called in sick. And the shift plan is still a WhatsApp message.
> Small hotels run on people. But they can't see the rush coming, and good staff burn out and leave.
>
> Meet HOI: Hospitality Operations Intelligence. One calm system for the whole hotel.
>
> It starts with the guest. Scan the QR in the room and ask by voice or touch, in Hindi or English: towels, a broken geyser, or a
> quiet place nearby, with the map one tap away.
>
> The request lands on the phone of the right person on shift. One tap to accept, one tap when done. If nobody answers, it moves on.
> When a guest checks out, cleaning is booked by itself. And staff can simply speak; it becomes a task.
>
> For the owner, one screen answers one question: is today OK? Rooms, cleaning, staff, guest requests and supplies, each with one next step.
>
> HOI predicts how full you'll be, weeks ahead, and builds fair shifts that keep rest rules. Tested on real hotel data, it cut forecast
> error by more than half.
>
> It also shows who may be thinking of leaving, and what to talk about. If the risk is high, it finds matching people on the state's
> travel and jobs site, or posts the job in one click.
>
> And every hotel joins a bigger network: a travel guide with verified stays, local experiences, crowd and road alerts, and seasonal jobs.
>
> Works on any phone. Speaks Hindi. No booking commission, ever.
>
> HOI. Smarter hotels. Happier teams.

## Production notes

**Recording the screens**
- Run `python scripts/wake_demo.py` first, so no screen shows a loading spinner.
- Record at 2560×1440 (desktop) and as a 390×844 phone. Export the film at 1920×1080.
- Use the demo accounts in `credentials.local.md`: guest page from Rooms & guests (room QR link); staff app as `HS05`; owner as `himsagar.owner`; travel guide at hoi-portal.vercel.app.
- Hide the browser bar and the mouse pointer. Use a soft highlight ring for taps instead.
- Record each action slowly, then speed it up in the edit (1.2–1.5×), so motion looks smooth.

**Look and sound**
- Background: off-white with soft blue-violet gradient glows, matching the logo. Device frames are thin and dark, with a soft shadow.
- Type: one clean sans-serif. Captions are 2–5 words. Highlight one key word in the HOI blue, as in the current video.
- Motion: slow push-ins (3–5%), 300–400 ms ease-out transitions, no spins or bounces.
- Music: soft piano with a light pulse, lifting at the reveal (0:20) and resolving at the end; −18 dB under the voice. A subtle tap or haptic sound on each button press.
- Voice: warm and unhurried. The Hindi phrase on screen in scene 9 should be heard clearly: record it separately with a native speaker if possible.

**Accuracy rules**
- Show staff as IDs (HS05) and role icons, never names or faces.
- Don't show money, revenue, energy or booking-system features: they are not in the product.
- "Cut forecast error by more than half": true for 7 to 30 days ahead on the public Portuguese hotel dataset (−51% to −63%). Keep the words "tested on real hotel data"; don't call it Uttarakhand data.
- Say "may be thinking of leaving", never "will quit".
- The demo hotel is made-up data: keep "(demo)" visible in the hotel name, or add "Demo data" in small type on the owner screens.

**Shorter cut (about 90 s):** drop scenes 8 and 16, and shorten scene 15 to "And every hotel joins Uttarakhand's travel and jobs network."

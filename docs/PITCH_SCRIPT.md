# HOI pitch: the whole project

**For:** the Team Innov8 presenter and Q&A team (AI-Tourism Hackathon 2026, TBI-GEU).
**Format:** a 3-minute spoken pitch with slide and demo cues, a 60-second version, the 6-part framework
the mentors asked for, and the answers for hard questions.
**Rule:** every number here comes from our own test results or a source listed in `docs/MARKET_AND_DIFFERENTIATION.md`.
Anything marked *(to validate)* is our belief, not yet proven with real hotels. Say so if asked.

## Part C · The framework (for the slides and the Q&A)

### 1. Customer vs user

| | Who | Can they buy it for themselves? |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer (pays)** | The hotel or homestay owner. Later: homestay clusters (8–10 homestays under one society, per the state's revised rules) and the state tourism department for the Travel Guide *(to validate)*. | **Yes.** An owner buys it for their own property, like a booking system. |
| **Users** | Owner and managers, department heads, frontline staff, guests, tourists, district officials, job seekers, staffing agencies. | **No.** They never pay. So it must be free for them, on any phone, with no app store and no training. |

### 2. Why, what, for whom

| | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Why** | Tourism here comes in waves (Char Dham, summer, festivals). Small properties plan staff by memory, paper or WhatsApp *(to validate)*, so peaks mean overwork, mistakes and people leaving. |
| **What** | One system that predicts the rush, plans fair shifts, sends every job to the right phone, proves the work with photos and times, and flags who needs a conversation. It's connected to a state travel guide with verified stays, local experiences and seasonal jobs. |
| **For whom** | Owners of small hotels (about 10–40 rooms) and registered homestays in Uttarakhand: more than 5,000 registered homestays, per a May 2026 news report of the state's figures. |

### 3. Everyone involved

| Stakeholder | What they need | What HOI gives them |
|---|---|---|
| Owner or GM | Know if today is OK; no surprises at peaks; keep good staff | Today page with one answer and five numbers; forecast and shift plan; "talk to them" prompts; English or Hindi |
| Department heads | Their team's jobs and people | Only their own department; pay reasons stay private |
| Frontline staff | Clear instructions, fair work, recognition | One job, one button, voice in Hindi; photos only where needed; thank-you points |
| Guest | Fast help without calling | Room QR: one-tap requests, Hindi voice, quiet places on the map, live status |
| Tourist | Where to go, how busy, where to stay safely | Travel guide: busy levels, road alerts, verified stays, local experiences, trip plans |
| Officials | Registrations, complaints, crowd warnings | Approvals, complaint queue, crowding forecast, road alerts, audit log |
| Job seekers and agencies | Seasonal work that fits their skills | Hiring board, skill matching, urgent requests from hotels |
| HOI team | Run many hotels safely | Console: hotels, plans, insights, activity log |

### 4. Pitch structure (one line each)

- **Problem:** small hotels can't see the rush, so they're short-staffed, staff leave and guests wait.
- **Solution:** predict, then plan, then act, then learn, across five simple apps, voice-first and in Hindi.
- **Customer vs user:** the owner pays; staff and guests use it free.
- **Moat:** see below.
- **Outcomes:** no surprise rush, no lost request, fewer people quitting in peak season, all measured.

### 5. Outcome-driven: prediction → insight → action → measured result

| Prediction (tested) | Insight | Action in HOI | How we measure it in a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rooms full in 1–30 days.** On the public Hotel Booking Demand data (two Portuguese hotels): 1 day ahead off by **1.9 vs 10.9 rooms (−83%)**; 7 days ahead **5.3 vs 10.9 (−51%)**. | "Next Saturday is the peak; housekeeping is one short in the morning" | Shift plan built from the forecast, keeping rest rules; one tap asks agencies for extra staff | Hours short per week; peak days fully covered; overtime hours |
| **Who may leave.** On 1,129 real employee records the model ranks a leaver above a stayer **66%** of the time vs **55%** for a checklist (modest, so it's a prompt, not a verdict). | "HS05 has worked many late shifts" | Talking points; "We talked" log; if high risk, matching people from the state jobs board, or the job posted in one click | Check-ins done; people who left during peak, before vs after |
| **Requests and work** | "Room 101 has waited 40 minutes" | Jobs routed to on-shift staff, re-offered if not taken; checkout creates cleaning; photo proof where needed | Minutes from request to done; jobs with proof; stock-outs per month |

### 6. Moat (why us, not eZee, STAAH or OYO)

1. **We run the stay; they sell the room.** Booking systems and OTAs own reservations. We found no public sign of forecast-driven shift planning, photo-checked housekeeping or turnover support in their products. So we sit next to them, not against them.
2. **Closed loop with each hotel's own data.** Forecast, then shifts, then jobs, then results flow back; every week makes that hotel's forecast better.
3. **Built for the frontline.** Voice in Hindi and English, one button per job, works on any phone through a link.
4. **The network.** Hotels, job seekers, staffing agencies and officials meet on one travel guide. More hotels bring more workers and agencies, which makes every hotel easier to staff.
5. **Trust by design.** Staff are IDs, never age, gender, religion or health; pay reasons stay with owner and HR; no booking commission.

*(Honest line if asked: "It's an early moat. It grows with each hotel's data and with the network.")*

### 7. Long-term business

| Stage | Who pays | Price *(proposal, to validate)* |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Registered homestays | **Free**, so adoption and data start |
| Grow | Small hotels, up to about 40 rooms | **About ₹999–1,999 per property per month**, near the cost of a small booking system |
| Scale | Hotel groups; homestay clusters sharing staff | Quote, or one shared subscription per cluster |
| Platform | State tourism department: travel guide, hiring board, crowd forecasts | Government contract *(to validate)* |

**Growth path:** Mussoorie and Rishikesh, then the Char Dham routes, then other hill states with the same seasonal pattern.
**Validation plan:** 8–10 interviews with go/no-go targets (for example, at least 2 of 3 managers willing to pay ₹999 a month), then a free 2-week pilot measuring the numbers in the table above.

## Part E · Hard questions: honest answers

| If they ask | Say |
|---|---|
| "Is this real hotel data?" | "The demo hotel is made-up data. The forecast was tested on a real public dataset of two Portuguese hotels. We have no Uttarakhand pilot yet; that's our next step." |
| "Has anyone agreed to pay?" | "Not yet. Prices are a proposal. We have an interview plan with go/no-go targets before we charge anyone." |
| "Why won't OYO or eZee add this?" | "They focus on selling rooms and owner revenue. Our edge is frontline voice in Hindi, a closed loop on each hotel's own data, and the link to the state's hiring network." |
| "Isn't predicting who leaves surveillance?" | "It uses work patterns only, like late shifts and missed breaks, never age, gender, religion or health. It gives a prompt to talk, department heads see only their team, and pay reasons stay with the owner and HR." |
| "What if staff don't use the app?" | "One button per job, voice in Hindi, no install, and photos only where they matter. Our usability test kit measures exactly this with five real people." |
| "What about bad internet in the hills?" | "Everything works through a web link on any phone, and the apps show a clear 'waking up' message instead of freezing. A fuller offline mode is on our list." |
| "Why would the state care?" | "Verified stays, faster approvals, crowding forecasts and a seasonal jobs pool, all fed by the hotels that use HOI." |
| "How will you reach hotels?" | "The district tourism office's list of registered homestays, the GEU hospitality faculty, and a free homestay tier." |
| "What does AI actually do here?" | "Three things: forecast how full you'll be, rank who may need a conversation, and understand voice requests in Hindi and English. Everything else is plain, reliable software." |
